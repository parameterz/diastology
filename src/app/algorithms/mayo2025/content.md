# Mayo Clinic 2025 Diastolic Function Algorithm

The Mayo Clinic algorithm represents an important contribution to our understanding of diastolic function assessment and its clinical significance, particularly concerning the prognostic implications of Grade 1 diastolic dysfunction (impaired relaxation pattern).

## Key Findings

This prospective study from the Mayo Clinic, using the Olmsted County Heart Function Study cohort followed for a median of 19.7 years, provides compelling evidence that:

* **Grade 1 diastolic dysfunction is common** (26% of subjects) in community-based populations, especially with advancing age
* **Increased mortality risk** is associated with Grade 1 diastolic dysfunction (HR 4.05, 95% CI 3.22-5.09)
* The association persists **even without other clinical or echocardiographic abnormalities** (HR 2.71, 95% CI 1.89-3.88)
* Grade 1 diastolic dysfunction is independently associated with **cardiovascular mortality** (HR 2.43, 95% CI 1.16-5.05)
* There is a significant association between Grade 1 diastolic dysfunction and **risk of death from dementia** (age- and sex-adjusted HR 2.30, 95% CI 1.54-3.45)

## Mayo Clinic Algorithm Structure

The Mayo Clinic algorithm maintains clear distinctions between normal diastolic function and Grade 1 diastolic dysfunction, differing from the 2016 ASE/EACVI guidelines. The approach involves:

* **First step: Assessment of LV filling pressures** using four criteria:
   - Septal e' velocity ≥ 7 cm/s (normal) or < 7 cm/s (abnormal)
   - E/e' ratio ≤ 15 (normal) or > 15 (abnormal)
   - TR velocity ≤ 2.8 m/s (normal) or > 2.8 m/s (abnormal)
   - LA volume index ≤ 34 mL/m² (normal) or > 34 mL/m² (abnormal)

* **Second step: Classification based on filling pressures and E/A ratio:**
   - Normal diastolic function: Normal filling pressures with E/A > 0.8
   - Grade 1 dysfunction: Normal filling pressures with E/A ≤ 0.8
   - Grade 2 dysfunction: Elevated filling pressures with E/A < 2
   - Grade 3 dysfunction: Elevated filling pressures with E/A ≥ 2

## Clinical Implications

This research challenges the notion that Grade 1 diastolic dysfunction is simply "normal for age" and highlights its importance as a biomarker of both cardiovascular and cognitive risk. Key implications include:

* **Reclassification concerns**: The 2016 ASE/EACVI guidelines may reclassify patients with isolated impaired relaxation as "normal," potentially missing an opportunity to identify increased risk
* **Heart failure prevention**: Identifying Grade 1 diastolic dysfunction early may provide opportunities for intervention before progression to clinical heart failure
* **Cognitive health connection**: The association with dementia mortality suggests important heart-brain axis considerations
* **Age is not the only factor**: While impaired relaxation becomes more common with age, many elderly subjects maintain normal diastolic filling patterns

This algorithm applies specifically to patients with preserved ejection fraction (≥50%) without clinical heart failure or significant valve disease.
